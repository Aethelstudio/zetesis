Rousseau's state of nature: history or thought experiment?

In the Discourse on Inequality (1755), Rousseau describes humans before society. The question is what kind of claim that description is: a report about the actual past, or a reasoned device for separating what is natural in us from what society adds.

Where interpretations sit

Hypothetical

Rousseau signals that his account is conditional reasoning, not a record of events. The state of nature isolates natural traits (self-preservation, pity) from acquired ones.

Historical

He draws on travellers' accounts of non-European peoples and narrates stages of development, which invites reading it as a claim about real early humans.

Conjectural history

A middle path: a plausible reconstruction of how things could have unfolded, valued for explanatory force rather than documentary proof.
